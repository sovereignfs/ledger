# Changelog

All notable changes to Ledger are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

Each release's section is also its GitHub release notes: the release workflow reads this file when a release is cut. Write the notes under
`## [Unreleased]` as you go; a release turns them into the version's section.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The foundation: settings declared once and checked at start-up (a wrong one stops the server, naming it and never its value); the database on three forms (an sqld service, a SQLite file or PostgreSQL) with migrations applied at start-up; background jobs in the web process; a nonce-based Content-Security-Policy and the usual headers; the app shell with its five places (a sidebar on a wide screen, a footer on a phone); browser tests in Chromium and WebKit; the manifest an instance reads, and one image with Compose setups for each database.
- Sign-in with a password, a passkey or an authenticator app, with email verification and reset when there is a mail server; the first account is the only one unless the operator opens sign-up. Settings (the profile, security, the theme, and deleting your account, which says what goes before it asks). A landing page for visitors, and Overview at the same address for someone signed in.
- A book for each person, which is everything of their budget, and the rule that a person reaches only the rows of their own book. Amounts as whole numbers of each currency's smallest unit, with every ISO 4217 currency and its own decimals.
- Exchange rates: a job fetches the day's rates against US dollars from Frankfurter for the currencies a book uses, and keeps one row per currency and day, from which the rate in force on any date is read. It asks for no currency by name, makes no request at all when only dollars are in use, and is turned off with `LEDGER_FX=off`.
- Conversion: an amount is converted at the rate in force on its own date, through US dollars, rounded half to even to the target currency's smallest unit, and a total says which amounts it left out for want of a rate instead of counting them as zero.
- Entries and their lines: one way to write an expense, an income, a transfer (in one currency or two), an opening balance, an adjustment or a new value, which refuses what does not add up in a sentence, and reads anything of another person's book as not there. An account's balance is the sum of its lines, never a stored number.
- Accounts: add a bank account, cash, savings, deposit, investment, property, credit card, loan or a person, each in one currency, with what is in it on a day. The Accounts screen shows your net worth in your base currency and says which amounts it left out for want of a rate; what you owe is said in words ("$420.00 used", "left to pay") and never with a minus. An account can be renamed, archived and brought back, and deleted while it has nothing but its opening balance.
- Categories and subcategories: make, rename, put in order, archive and delete them from the Budget. Something that has been spent on is archived and not deleted, so it stays in past months, and a new expense cannot be put on an archived subcategory.
- The add dialog: **Add** is on every page and opens a form for an expense or an income (a drawer on a phone). Enter saves, "Save and add another" keeps the date and account, a date in another month says which month it counts towards, and a save that is refused opens the form again with what you typed and the reason. An entry is changed or deleted from the same form.
- Splits: one payment can be spread across several subcategories (or income sources). The dialog shows what is left to place and will not save until the parts add up.
- Payees: type who you paid when you add an entry and it is remembered, offered as you type, with the subcategory and account you last used with it. Payees can be renamed, archived and merged (every entry moves to the one you keep), and each shows its totals by month.
- Transfers and refunds: move money between your accounts (pay a card from checking, lend to a person), in one currency or two; between two currencies you enter what left and what arrived, and the rate is shown. A refund puts money back in an account and takes it off the subcategory.
- Transactions: every entry, newest first and paged by date, with filters (account, category, payee, type, status, dates, and text in a payee or note) that live in the address, so a view can be bookmarked and reopens the same. With one account chosen, each row shows that account's balance after it. Several entries can be marked cleared or pending, moved to another subcategory, or deleted at once.
- Status and reconciling: an entry can be turned between pending and cleared by hand. Reconcile an account to its statement: it shows what is different before it does anything, marks every entry up to the statement's day cleared, and records the difference as an adjustment. Reconciling again to the same balance records nothing.
- `pnpm dev` now starts on a fresh checkout: the first time it makes a `.env.local` for the machine (a SQLite file in `./data`, local sign-in with a secret made there), which git does not track and which is never overwritten.
- Value of an investment or a property: give it a value on a day and the change is recorded as a change in value, which is neither income nor spending. Its page keeps the history, and says before it records anything what the change will be.
- Budget history: a budgeted amount is kept by month, so raising it from March leaves February as it was; a change is from this month or from next, two changes in a month keep the later, and a budget can end.
- Income: add income sources (the first is the primary, which cannot be deleted; make another primary to swap), set what each is budgeted from this month or next, and see a month's budgeted and received income apart, converted to your base currency with any missing rate said. Income entries name a source.
- Budget: a month's budgeted and spent for each subcategory, in Bills, Spending and Saving, with a bar and "over by" in words, totals in your base currency with any missing rate said, and a month picker. Open a row to change what it is budgeted from this month or from next, see its history and its entries this month; a split counts in each part's row and a foreign expense is converted at its own date.
- Setup: a new budget starts with a four-step wizard at `/setup`: your currency (guessed from your browser's locale), your first account and what is in it, your main income, and the first categories as chips you tap, each with a monthly amount. Each step is saved as you go, going back makes nothing twice, nothing is made that you did not tap, and setup can be skipped.
- Overview: the page you land on shows this month's income received, spending, and what you have saved against what you planned, your net worth, the budget rows closest to or over their budget, recent transactions, and a checklist of what is left to set up (which you can put away), from which you can add an entry.
- The setup checklist on Overview now links to Bills and to Accounts (where a jar is made) and ticks them once you have a bill or a jar; the landing page lists what the app does today.
- Sovereign's notices: when a person signs out at their Sovereign instance, they are signed out here, and when they delete their account there, it is deleted here, with everything in it. A notice is checked against Sovereign's keys and is never acted on twice.
- Sign-in through a Sovereign instance: with `LEDGER_CORE_URL`, `LEDGER_CORE_CLIENT_ID` and `LEDGER_CORE_CLIENT_SECRET` set, people can sign in through it (instead of, or as well as, a password), and an account made with a password can link it from Settings. A login is matched to an account by its Sovereign id and never by its email address.
- Ledger can be installed as an app from the browser (a web manifest, icons and a service worker). The service worker keeps only the app's own files and icons, never a page with your money on it, and says Ledger needs a connection when there is none.
- Export and import: Settings > Data downloads your whole book as one file (accounts, entries, budgets, bills, jars, reviews and settings; not the exchange rates, and nothing you sign in with) and imports such a file into an empty book, checking it whole first, so a file that is not right changes nothing.
- The book's settings: the name, base currency, time zone, first day of the week and number format can be changed from Settings, with the currencies the book uses and whether the monthly recap is wanted. Changing the base currency or the time zone changes nothing already entered.
- Insights: two things worth a look, worked out when a page needs them: a subcategory over its budget two months running, and an expense of more than three times the usual for its subcategory. Overview shows at most three, a month's report all of them.
- Month-end review: a past month without a review "needs review", Overview shows a banner for the most recent, and a month's report can be marked reviewed (and undone), with a link to adjust the next month's budget.
- Net worth over time: what you were worth at each month end, assets and liabilities apart, converted at each day's rates, on Accounts and in Reports. Nothing is stored: it is worked out from the entries, an opening, a new value and an adjustment included.
- Reports for a year: the months added up, each against the budget it had then and at the rates of its own days, by category and subcategory and month by month.
- Reports: a list of months with what was received, spent and saved, and a report for each month: income and spending by category against the budget, the three savings figures (planned, saved, and saved after what went into jars), and the cards, people and loans. A loan's interest is spending and its principal is not.
- A development seed, `pnpm seed`: a year of realistic data (accounts of every kind in three currencies, bills that post themselves and one that asks first, a loan paying itself down, jars, splits, transfers) for the book of the account that has signed in, to look at every screen with something in it. It refuses a database that is not on this machine.
- Spending from a jar: an expense from an account with jars can be paid from one in the add dialog, which shows what each holds and refuses more than that before it is sent. The expense releases the same amount, and changing or deleting it changes or gives back the jar.
- Monthly jar contributions: each jar's contribution is earmarked once a month by the book's own calendar, never partly. One that does not fit in what is free is skipped, and Overview says so; it also says when spending has left an account holding less than its jars.
- Saving jars: money set aside inside a bank, cash, savings or deposit account for a purpose, with a target and a monthly contribution. Earmark and release by hand, never past what the account holds and never below nothing, even with two at once. The account shows what is in jars and what is free, and the Saving group of the Budget shows each jar's balance and progress.
- Loan instalments: a loan can pay itself from an account every month, each instalment split into interest (from the yearly rate on what is left, by days) and what comes off the loan, with the last one capped so the loan ends at exactly zero. Making them makes a Loans category and the loan's interest subcategory.
- Bills in the budget: a subcategory can be budgeted by its recurring bills, so a yearly 600 insurance budgets 50 a month while its payment lands in the month paid. Making, editing or ending a bill updates the budget from the current month on and never changes a past one.
- Upcoming bills: a bill set to ask first waits on Overview and the transactions list, overdue ones included, to post as it is, post with the amount it came to, or skip. A second click, or a second window, posts nothing more.
- Recurring entries: make an entry repeat from its dialog (Repeat…) and a Bills page shows what repeats, its next day and whether it posts itself or asks first, to switch or end. A job posts what is due each hour in the book's own time zone, catches up days a server was down, and never posts one occurrence twice, even run twice or from two processes.
- The repeat rule for recurring entries: every so many days, weeks, months or years from a start day, optionally until an end, with the 31st falling on the last day of a shorter month and a leap day on the 28th in other years, and what a bill comes to in a month for the budget.
