# Epic 4: The ledger

## Status

⏳ In progress: 7 of 9 tasks are done.

## Overview

Money accounts whose balances are derived, transactions made of lines that add up, and everything a person does with them day to day. This is what
the plugin did not have: 4.2 is the centre, and its invariants are held by every later write.

## Related

[`DESIGN.md`](../../DESIGN.md) §6, §14.4, §18.3

## Tasks

#### ✅ 4.1 — Money accounts

**Goal:** every kind of account, with its opening balance. **Design:** §6.1, §6.4. **Depends on:** 3.1, 3.2, 4.2.

**Technical:** `money_account` (**the table was made in 4.2**) and `loan`; the nine types and their sides; making one writes an `opening` transaction; edit name and institution,
archive, delete only with nothing but its opening; `/accounts` grouped by side with net worth (in the base currency through 3.4); `/accounts/<id>`
with its entries, credit used and available, a loan's left and paid, a person's owes-you or you-owe.

**Accepts when:** a new account shows its opening balance; an account with entries cannot be deleted but can be archived and stays in past totals;
the person's words never show a minus for money owed.

**Done as:** `src/ledger/`: `account-types.ts` (the nine types with their sides, and pure checks that refuse in sentences: a name, a currency, a limit only on a card, loan terms only on a loan, an end that is not before the start, a rate as decimal text), `accounts.ts` (`createAccount` writes the account, adds its currency to `book_currency`, writes a loan's terms and the opening balance **as an `opening` entry through 4.2's one path, in the same database transaction** (`runWrite` and `insertTransaction` are exported for this), so an account never exists without the balance it was given; `updateAccount` changes the name, institution, a card's limit and a loan's lender, end, instalment and rate, never the currency or kind; `setArchived`; `deleteAccount`, whose own statement refuses while any entry but an opening has a line on the account, deleting the opening and the loan with it; `listAccounts`, `getAccount` with balance, loan terms and `deletable`; `entriesOf`). **`loan`** is a new table (migration `0005_loan`, in `BOOK_TABLES`, seeded by the deletion test). `present.ts`: **a balance is said in words, never with a minus for money owed** ("$420.00 used", "left to pay", "You owe $5.00", "overdrawn"), a card's used and available, a loan's left and paid. **Routes** `/api/accounts` (GET, POST) and `/api/accounts/[id]` (GET, PATCH with `archived`, DELETE, 409 with a sentence for an account that has been used), in `routes.ts`; what they do is in `src/access/accounts-api.ts`, with the body read by `account-input.ts` (types checked, unknown fields ignored, capped at 16 KiB) so that `accounts-api.test.ts` calls every one **as the second person**: a 404 with nothing in it, nothing changed, the same answer as for an account that never was. **Screens:** `/accounts` (net worth in the base currency at today's rates through 3.4, with "N amounts in XXX not included" and, with no base currency yet, one total for each currency; the accounts in what you have, what you owe and people; `?archived=1`), the Add account dialog (what is owed is typed as an amount, and the sign is made once, in the dialog), and `/accounts/[id]` (the summary, a card's limit, used and available, a loan's terms, the entries newest first, rename, archive or bring back, delete only while it is allowed). `todayIn(timeZone)` in `src/dates.ts` gives the book's own today. 8 browser tests across Chromium and WebKit: an account with its opening balance, a card read as "$420.00 used" with no minus, archive and bring back and delete, and a refusal shown in the dialog. Left out: a loan's interest subcategory is accepted by the API and checked to be the book's but the dialog does not offer it (5.1 gives it something to choose); an account's page is not paged beyond its latest 100 entries (the transactions list, 4.7); a person's opening direction is chosen in the dialog and is not changeable after, as an entry (4.3 edits it).

#### ✅ 4.2 — Transactions and lines

**Goal:** the one write path, and its invariants. **Design:** §6.2, §6.3, §6.8. **Depends on:** 3.1, 3.2.

**Technical:** `txn`, `txn_line`; `src/ledger/` validators for each type (which targets, signs, exactly one target per line, lines add up to zero in
the currency, the transfer exception records two amounts), every target the same book's in the statement; create, edit (lines replaced in one
transaction) and delete; balances as sums, by date; the property test of random sequences of writes.

**Accepts when:** a transaction whose lines do not add up is refused with a sentence; a line pointing at another book's subcategory reads as not
found; after a thousand random writes every balance equals its lines.

**Done as:** `src/ledger/`. **Tables** (migration `0004_transactions`, both dialects): `txn`, `txn_line`, and, because every line points at one and "the same book's, in the statement" cannot be held against a table that is not there, **`money_account`, `category`, `subcategory` and `income_source`** with the columns `DESIGN.md` §18.3 gives them; what a person can do with them (make, edit, archive, the screens) stays with 4.1, 5.1 and 5.3, which now add logic and no migration for these. All six are in `BOOK_TABLES` (in the order of deletion) and `people.test.ts` now seeds a row of each for two people and fails for a table of a book that it did not seed. `payee_id` and `recurring_id` with `occurrence_date` (unique together) are columns for 4.5 and 6.2; nothing writes them yet. **`validate.ts`**, pure: a line names exactly one of an account, a subcategory, an income source or equity; an amount is a non-zero whole number in a currency that is one; each type has the targets it may (an expense an account that paid and subcategories, and a second account only on a loan or a credit card, so a loan instalment is one entry; an income one account and income sources; a transfer two different accounts; an opening, an adjustment and a new value one account and its own equity, and a new value only on an investment or a property); **the lines add up to zero in the entry's currency**, the transfer of two currencies being the one exception, whose two lines are one down and one up. Each refusal is a sentence ("The lines add up to -1.00 USD, and they must add up to nothing: …") that names no one's thing. **`transactions.ts`** is the one path: `createTransaction`, `updateTransaction` (the lines replaced in one transaction with the entry's own row; a status not given is kept) and `deleteTransaction` (entry and lines together; safe to repeat). The shape is checked first; then the entry's row is written under the permission (`inBook`), then every account, subcategory and income source the lines name is found in the book (anything not found, or another person's, is `not_found`), then the accounts' types and currencies are checked, then each line is written by a statement that again requires the entry to be the account's and its target the same book's; a refusal anywhere rolls the whole write back. **`balances.ts`:** a balance is the sum of an account's lines on or before a date, never written, and another person's account has none. **`invariants.ts`:** `findViolations` asks the rows themselves (one target a line, every entry adds up, two currencies only in a transfer of two lines, no entry without lines or line without entry). **The property test:** a thousand seeded random writes (every type, edits, deletions, and writes that must be refused), a model kept in the test, balances equal to the model's at every hundredth write and on four dates at the end, no violation, and the other person's book untouched. Each key test was seen to fail against the code it guards (the same-book check dropped, the sum check dropped, an edit that keeps the old lines, a delete that keeps them, balances that ignore the date, no permission on a delete); the permission on the entry's own `INSERT` and `UPDATE` is **a second line of defence that no outside call can observe**, since the read of the targets that follows is under it too, so dropping either alone changes no answer. `isCalendarDay` moved to `src/dates.ts`. Left out: no route or server action yet (the add dialog, 4.3, adds them with their `routes.ts` entries and second-person tests); a write to an **archived** account is not refused here (the pickers hide them, 4.3); a loan's line must be in the paying account's currency (one currency to an entry).

#### ✅ 4.3 — The add dialog

**Goal:** logging in seconds, from anywhere. **Design:** §14.4. **Depends on:** 4.1, 5.1.

**Technical:** `Dialog` wide and `Drawer` narrow, one form: type, amount (`MoneyInput` in the account's currency), account, subcategory or income
source, date (today), note; Enter saves; "Save and add another"; applied at once and undone on a refusal; a note when the date counts towards another
month; edit and delete from the same dialog. Remembers the last account.

**Accepts when:** an expense is logged with the keyboard alone; a refused save restores the list and says why; reopening shows a blank form.

**Done as:** `src/entries/`. `EntryProvider` (client) holds the one form and is mounted in the signed-in layout, so **Add**, in the sidebar and in the phone's More drawer where the shell had drawn it disabled, opens it from any page, and an entry's **Edit** (on an account's page) opens the same form. `EntrySurface` is the one place that asks the width, a `Dialog` on a wide screen and a `Drawer` on a narrow one with the same content, and `no-fork.test.ts` lists it as rendering only. The form is an Expense or an Income: amount (`MoneyInput` in the chosen account's currency), account, subcategory or income source, date (the book's today), note. `compose.ts` (pure) turns it into the two lines (an expense: account down, subcategory up; an income: account up, income source down) and says what is missing in order; `editable` and `formOf` read an entry back, and an entry with parts, two accounts, an original amount or another kind is told so and offered Delete only until 4.4 and 4.6. Enter saves; **Save and add another** keeps the date and account and clears the rest; **a save closes the dialog at once, and a refusal opens it again with what was typed and the sentence** (Edit and Delete the same, Delete asking twice); the last account is remembered in `localStorage` and reopening is otherwise blank; `monthNote` says "This counts towards March 2020." for a date in another month. Server: `/api/entry-options` (the person's live accounts of a kind that holds money or credit, live categories with live subcategories, live income sources, today on the book's clock), `/api/transactions` (POST) and `/api/transactions/[id]` (GET, PATCH, DELETE), in `routes.ts`, with their handlers in `src/access/transactions-api.ts` and the body read by `txn-input.ts`, called as the second person: 404 with nothing in it for another person's entry, and nothing changes. `getEntry` and `loadEntryOptions` are in `src/ledger/entries.ts`. 8 browser tests across Chromium and WebKit: an expense logged with typing and Enter from the Budget and seen on the account, a blank reopen with the account remembered, a refusal (the subcategory archived while the dialog is open) that reopens with the note and amount kept, and a month note, an edit and a delete. Each key test was seen to fail against the code it guards (the income sign, a read with no permission, archived accounts offered). Left out: the payee (4.5), splits (4.4), transfers (4.6), "from a jar" (7.3), a negative expense for a refund (4.6), and selecting by keyboard in the selects in the browser test (it picks them and types the rest).

#### ✅ 4.4 — Splits

**Goal:** one payment across several subcategories or income sources. **Design:** §6.2, §14.4. **Depends on:** 4.3.

**Technical:** "Split" in the dialog adds lines; the remainder is shown until they add up; the list shows "Split" and the parts on hover and in detail;
each part counts towards its own subcategory's spent.

**Accepts when:** 84.20 split 61.00 and 23.20 lands in both subcategories; saving with a remainder is refused before submitting.

**Done as:** the add dialog's form (`src/entries/compose.ts`) now has a **Split**: the whole amount, and parts that each name a subcategory (or an income source) and an amount. `remainder` is what is left of the amount; the dialog shows "$23.20 left to place", "$1.80 over" or "The parts add up." as parts are typed, and `missing` refuses **before anything is sent**, in a sentence ("The parts are 3.20 USD short: they must add up to the amount."), also asking for at least two parts and a target and an amount for each. `compose` makes the account's line for the whole and one line for each part, in the account's currency (an income's parts are negative), so the one write path's sum check holds them again on the server, which a test shows by refusing a short split. An entry with several parts is editable in the same dialog, which reads it back as a split (`editable` and `formOf`); a mixture of targets, a second account or an original amount still is not. The account's page marks such an entry "Split in 2" (`parts` in `EntryOfAccount`); the transactions list (4.7) will show "Split" with the parts. 84.20 as 61.00 and 23.20 lands in both subcategories: seen in the lines the entry reads back, and in the browser, where the account is down by 84.20. Each key test was seen to fail against the code it guards (the remainder ignored, an income's parts not negated). Left out: per-part spent figures, which are the budget's (5.4), and a part's own note.

#### ✅ 4.5 — Payees

**Goal:** who was paid, remembered. **Design:** §6.5. **Depends on:** 4.3.

**Technical:** `payee`; made on first use, autocomplete (`SuggestionInput`), the last subcategory and account proposed; rename, merge (lines move in
one transaction), archive; `/payees/<id>` with totals by month.

**Accepts when:** typing a known payee proposes its last subcategory; merging two payees leaves every transaction on the survivor.

**Done as:** `payee` (migration `0006_payee`, in `BOOK_TABLES`, seeded by the deletion test) with **one column more than `DESIGN.md` §18.3 had, `name_key`**: the name trimmed, its inner spaces made one and lowercased, unique with `book_id`, so that "Corner Shop" and "corner shop" are one payee and **two writes that make it at once make one row** (a test makes four at the same moment); §18.3 says so. `src/ledger/payee-write.ts` is what the one write path needs: `resolvePayee` finds the payee a name means or makes it (the insert carries the permission and the unique key, and an archived payee is brought back by typing its name) and `rememberPayee` keeps the last subcategory and account; `TxnInput.payee` is a name, and **on a change, not given keeps the entry's payee, a name changes it and `null` removes it**. The payee is made in the entry's own transaction, so an entry that is refused leaves none behind. `src/ledger/payees.ts`: `listPayees` (by name, or by part of a name in any case, where `%` and `_` are only themselves), `getPayee`, `renamePayee` (a name another payee has says to merge instead), `setPayeeArchived`, `mergePayees` (every entry moves to the survivor and the other goes, in one transaction; both must be the account's and different) and `payeeMonths` (spent and received by month and currency). Routes `/api/payees` (GET, with `?q=` for the autocomplete) and `/api/payees/[id]` (GET with its months; PATCH `name`, `archived` or `mergeInto`), in `routes.ts`, called as the second person: a 404 with nothing in it, nothing changes. The add dialog has a Payee field (`PayeeInput` on the design system's `SuggestionInput`, asking the server a moment after typing stops, whose own text is always a valid answer): **picking a known payee proposes the subcategory and account last used with it**, which stay changeable; a new name is made on save. `/payees` lists, renames, archives and merges (asking twice) and has `?archived=1`; `/payees/[id]` shows its totals by month; both are linked from the Budget; an account's page shows the payee on each entry. 4 browser tests across Chromium and WebKit. Each key test was seen to fail against the code it guards (a merge that leaves entries, names that are case-sensitive, a merge with no permission). Left out: merging from the payee's own page, and a payee's total in the base currency (a month is shown in each currency it was spent in, which needs no rates).

#### ✅ 4.6 — Transfers

**Goal:** money between accounts, in one or two currencies. **Design:** §6.2, §6.8. **Depends on:** 4.3.

**Technical:** the transfer form: from, to, amount, and a second amount when the currencies differ, showing the implied rate; card payments, lending
to and receiving from a person; a refund as a negative expense.

**Accepts when:** paying a card from checking changes both balances and no spending figure; a EUR→USD transfer keeps both amounts and shows their
rate.

**Done as:** the add dialog has a **Transfer** beside Expense and Income: From, To, the amount, and, **when the two accounts are in different currencies, a second amount for what arrived**, with the rate they imply ("1 USD = 0.9 EUR") shown as it is typed. `src/entries/rate.ts` works the rate in integers, as the ratio of what arrived to what left with each currency's own decimals, in millionths rounded half to even and written without trailing zeros; it is shown and never stored (`DESIGN.md` §6.8). `compose` makes the two lines (the one account down, the other up; both amounts when the currencies differ), `missing` says what is wanted before anything is sent (two different accounts, the amount, and the amount that arrived), and a transfer carries no payee. A transfer can be **between any two live accounts**, so `entry-options` offers `transferAccounts` (a card, a loan or a person's account included, which an expense is not on): paying a card from checking, lending to a person and being paid back are transfers, which touch no subcategory and so no spending figure, as the entry read back shows. **A refund is an expense the other way round**: a checkbox in the expense form puts the account up and the subcategory (or each part of a split) down; an entry like that opens as a refund. A transfer opens in the same dialog to change or delete. API test: a card paid from checking moves both balances, the entry has no subcategory or income line, a USD→EUR transfer keeps 100.00 and 90.00, and one with both amounts going the same way, or an amount in the wrong currency for its account, is refused. 6 browser tests across Chromium and WebKit (a card paid and read as "$170.00 used", a USD→EUR transfer that is refused without what arrived, shows the rate and reopens with both amounts, and a refund). Each key test was seen to fail against the code it guards (the refund's sign, both currencies given the sent amount, the rate ignoring decimals). Left out: a person's "owes you" and "you owe" as words on the transfer form (the account's page says it), and the note on the date counting towards another month does not apply differently to a transfer.

#### ✅ 4.7 — The transactions list

**Goal:** every entry, findable. **Design:** §6.9. **Depends on:** 4.4, 4.5, 4.6.

**Technical:** `/transactions`, newest first, paged by date; filters in the address (account, category or subcategory, payee, type, status, date
range, text in payee and note, matched as text, never a pattern); a running balance with one account filtered; bulk clear, recategorise, delete.

**Accepts when:** each filter and their combinations return the right rows on all three databases; an address with filters reopens the same view.

**Done as:** **`/transactions`**. `src/transactions/filter.ts` (pure) reads and writes the filters in the address (account, category, subcategory, payee, type, status, a range of days, text), ignoring what is not a valid value and never failing a page; `filterQuery` writes them in a fixed order and each is the other's inverse, so **one view has one address**, and the page sends what the form posted (empty fields, a category written `c:` or `s:`) to the tidy one. `src/ledger/entry-list.ts`: `listEntries` is **newest first by date, then when made, then id, paged by a cursor** in that order ("Older entries", itself in the address) so that nothing is missed or repeated, with the filters as bound conditions that combine; a category takes in its subcategories and a subcategory is narrower and wins; **text is matched as text** (what is typed is lowercased and escaped, so `%` and `_` and `\` are themselves and nothing typed is a pattern or reaches the statement as anything but a value), against the payee's name and the note; with exactly one account filtered each row also carries **that account's balance right after it**, which stays the account's true balance whatever else is filtered out of view. `src/transactions/present.ts` (pure) reads a row: the payee, note or kind; the subcategory with its category, the income source, **"Split"** (with the parts for a hover), "Transfer", "Opening balance"; the account, or "From → To"; the movement, on the one account filtered if there is one, with what arrived when a transfer is between two currencies. `src/ledger/bulk.ts`: **mark cleared or pending, move to another subcategory, delete**, at most 200 at a time, each statement carrying the permission; an id that is not the account's changes nothing and is counted as skipped; only an entry with exactly one subcategory line is moved (a split has its parts chosen one by one), to a subcategory that is the book's and live; a delete takes the entries with their lines in one transaction. `POST /api/transactions/bulk` is in `routes.ts` and called as the second person. The screen: a filter form that asks with `GET`, the table with a box on each row and a bar of what can be done to the chosen ones (delete asks twice), and **Open** on a row, which opens the add dialog to change or delete it. Run on all three databases: the dataset of eight entries gives each filter and their combinations the right rows, paging in threes with nothing missed or repeated, the running balances, and another person listing nothing even naming Ada's account and category. 6 browser tests across Chromium and WebKit (filters, the address reopening the same view, a `%` that is only a `%`, the running balance, and the bulk actions). Each key test was seen to fail against the code it guards (text not escaped, a cursor that repeats, a running balance that drops the later-made, a bulk status with no permission, a move that takes splits). Left out: the payee filter has no control on the form (it comes from a link, `?payee=<id>`, which the payees page can offer); a day's entries are paged whole, not split across pages mid-day other than by the cursor; and the transactions' status by hand on a single row (4.8 toggles it).

#### 📋 4.8 — Status and reconciling

**Goal:** a balance that matches the bank's. **Design:** §6.4, §6.7. **Depends on:** 4.7.

**Technical:** `status` pending or cleared, toggled by hand; Reconcile on an account: statement balance and date, the difference from cleared entries
to that date, confirm writes an `adjustment` (if not zero) and clears the pending entries to the date, in one transaction.

**Accepts when:** reconciling to a statement 1.20 above records an adjustment of 1.20 and clears every entry to the date; reconciling again to the
same figure records nothing.

#### 📋 4.9 — Revaluation

**Goal:** what an investment or a property is worth. **Design:** §6.6. **Depends on:** 4.1.

**Technical:** "Value on date" on investment and property accounts writes a `revaluation` of the difference; their page shows the value history; not
income, not spending.

**Accepts when:** valuing a fund at 10,500 from 10,000 raises net worth by 500 and changes no report's income or spent.
